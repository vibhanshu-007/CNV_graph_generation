# Copy Number Variation (CNV)

## What is CNV?
Copy Number Variations (CNVs) are a type of **structural variation** in the genome where sections of DNA are **duplicated** (gain) or **deleted** (loss).  
These variations can range from **1 kilobase (kb) to several megabases (Mb)** in length and may involve one or multiple genes.

---

## Types of CNVs
1. **Deletions** – Loss of a DNA segment.  
2. **Duplications** – Extra copies of a DNA segment.  
3. **Multiallelic CNVs** – Variations where more than two copy number states exist.  
4. **Complex CNVs** – Rearrangements involving multiple breakpoints (e.g., both deletions and duplications).

---

## Detection Methods
- **Array-Based Methods**  
  - Comparative Genomic Hybridization (aCGH)  
  - SNP Arrays  

- **Sequencing-Based Methods**  
  - Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS)  
  - Whole Exome Sequencing (WES)  
  - Targeted NGS Panels  

- **Computational Tools**  
  - CNVnator, Control-FREEC, GATK gCNV, etc.  

---

## Biological and Clinical Importance
- **Genetic Diversity**  
  - Contributes to differences in phenotype between individuals.  

- **Disease Associations**  
  - Linked with neurodevelopmental disorders (e.g., autism, schizophrenia).  
  - Implicated in cancer (oncogene amplifications or tumor suppressor deletions).  

- **Pharmacogenomics**  
  - CNVs in drug-metabolizing enzyme genes can influence drug response.  

---

## Challenges
- Accurate detection in repetitive or GC-rich regions.  
- Distinguishing **benign polymorphisms** from **pathogenic CNVs**.  
- Need for high-quality algorithms and validation.  

---

## Summary
CNVs are **large-scale genetic variations** involving gains or losses of DNA segments.  
They play a **critical role in evolution, human diversity, and disease**, and modern sequencing plus computational approaches have made their detection more precise.
